India Meteorological Department (IMD) Cyclone Intensity Scale

How tropical atmospheric disturbances in the North Indian Ocean (Bay of Bengal & Arabian Sea) evolve from calm depressions into super cyclones.

Speed Conversion:

1 Knot = 1.852 km/h

Stage 0 < 17 knots

Low Pressure Area (LPA)

< 31 km/h

🍃 Everyday Analogy:

A gentle sea breeze with cloudy skies. Air pressure begins to drop; water vapor starts to gather. Not a storm yet.

Stage 1 17 – 27 knots

Depression (D)

31 – 49 km/h

🌧️ Everyday Analogy:

A rainy, gusty monsoon day. Walking with an umbrella is hard as it flips inside-out. Fishermen advised against deep-sea fishing.

Stage 2 28 – 33 knots

Deep Depression (DD)

50 – 61 km/h

🛵 Everyday Analogy:

Riding a two-wheeler on an open highway. Strong crosswinds push against vehicles; tree limbs shake violently. Rotating vortex tightens.

Naming Milestone 34 – 47 knots

Cyclonic Storm (CS)

62 – 88 km/h

🌀 Milestone Event:

Storm officially receives its NAME (e.g., ASNA, FENGAL). Thatched roofs blow off; coastal small boats risk capsizing.

Stage 4 48 – 63 knots

Severe Cyclonic Storm (SCS)

89 – 117 km/h

⚠️ Evacuation Threshold:

Equal to high-speed train wind. Medium trees uprooted, tin sheds ripped away, power lines severed. Coastal evacuations ordered.

Cat 1-2 Hurricane 64 – 89 knots

Very Severe Cyclone (VSCS)

118 – 166 km/h

👁️ Clear Eye Visible:

Distinct circular eye appears on satellite. Massive trees down, telecom towers damaged. (e.g. MURASU 2026 at 84 kt).

Cat 3-4 Hurricane 90 – 119 knots

Extremely Severe (ESCS)

167 – 221 km/h

🌪️ Major Disaster:

Massive destructive force. Concrete structures damaged; power grids destroyed; seawater floods inland. (e.g. ARNAB 2026 at 119 kt, BIPARJOY).

Cat 5 Cataclysm ≥ 120 knots

Super Cyclonic Storm (SuCS)

≥ 222 km/h

🌊 Total Devastation:

Highest possible meteorological severity. Total annihilation of buildings, 4-6m storm surges submerging coast. (e.g. 1999 Odisha Super Cyclone, AMPHAN 2020).

Why Is It Called a "Depression"?

In meteorology, the word "depression" literally means a dip or drop in atmospheric air pressure. When warm tropical sea surface water heats the air, that air rises rapidly, creating a partial vacuum (a "depression") near the sea surface. Surrounding air rushes inwards to fill this vacuum, and Earth's Coriolis rotation twists it into a vortex.

When Does a Storm Get a Name?

Storms do not get names while they are Depressions or Deep Depressions (they are given temporary technical codes like BOB-01). Only when sustained eyewall winds reach 34 knots (62 km/h) does the IMD assign an official name chosen from the regional pool (provided by 13 member nations including India, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, and Oman).

Holland Pressure Driving Law

The lower the central pressure drops, the more violent the eyewall winds become:
• Normal Sea Level: 1010 hPa (calm 15 kt)
• Cyclonic Storm: ~990 hPa (45 kt)
• Very Severe (MURASU): 973 hPa (84 kt)
• Extremely Severe (ARNAB): 940 hPa (119 kt)
